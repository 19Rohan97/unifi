FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What type of network cable should I install: Cat5e, Cat6, Cat6A, or Cat7?

Cat6 is the most common choice for most Michigan businesses because it supports modern networks and many PoE devices. Cat6A is ideal when you want full 10Gb support over longer runs, and Cat7 is best for specialized or high-interference environments.

How do you decide how many drops and data ports a building needs?

We plan drops based on workstation count, WiFi access point coverage, cameras, access control doors, printers, conference rooms, and future expansion. After a walkthrough, we create a layout that balances performance, coverage, and budget.

Do you install cabling for WiFi access points, cameras, and access control?

Yes. We install structured cabling for wireless access points, security cameras, intercoms, door controllers, and other PoE devices, with proper pathways, labeling, and testing.

Do you upgrade or replace existing cabling in older buildings?

Yes. We can replace outdated cable, expand your network, or re-terminate and clean up existing runs when possible.

Can you run cable in active facilities without disrupting operations?

Yes. We can work around business hours, after hours, or in phases to reduce disruption.

Do you install plenum-rated, riser-rated, or outdoor-rated cable?

Yes. We install CMP (plenum), CMR (riser), and outdoor or direct-burial cable based on building requirements and installation conditions.

Do you provide cable testing and certification reports?

Yes. We test for continuity, performance, and standards compliance, and can provide certification results and documentation when required.

Do you label cables and provide documentation after installation?

Yes. We label both ends, organize patch panels, and provide port mapping or as-built documentation as needed.

Can your cabling support PoE devices and higher power needs?

Yes. We design cabling to support PoE devices like cameras, access points, phones, and door controllers.

How much does network cabling installation cost in Michigan?

Cost depends on the number of drops, cable type, pathway access, ceiling height, wall construction, testing and certification requirements, and whether racks and patch panels are included.
